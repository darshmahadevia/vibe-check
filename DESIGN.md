# A soundtrack for the moment

Vibe Check is a dark music workbench. The upload and recommendation flow stays central. The visual reference is a Spotify-inspired library with fully visible artwork, a separate thumbnail selector, and clear mood comparisons against a pitch-black ground.

## Palette and typography

Use pitch black `#000000` for the page, `#121212` for main surfaces, and `#181818` for raised or selected surfaces. Primary text is white `#ffffff`; secondary text is neutral gray `#b3b3b3`, with quieter text at `#a7a7a7`. Dividers use `#303030`. Spotify green `#1ed760` carries the headline highlight, photo icon, song bars, selection borders, links, checkboxes, and focus rings. Error text uses `#ffb3bb` on a muted red surface. Use flat surfaces without ambient gradients.

All action buttons share a `#181818` fill, white text, a fine `#303030` border, a 10-pixel corner radius, a minimum height of 46 pixels, and the same typography. This includes navigation, upload, camera, Find my song, queue, batch selection, and reset controls. Hover lightens the surface and border. Keyboard focus uses green. Disabled controls use a darker surface, muted text, and the native disabled attribute. Content selection buttons retain their artwork or ranking layouts with the same corners and neutral surfaces; their selected border is green. Notification dismissal uses the same treatment in a 46-pixel square.

Manrope is self-hosted through `next/font/google`. Headings use medium weight, a compact line height, and tracking no tighter than `-0.04em`. Body text has generous leading. Numeric comparisons use tabular figures. No eyebrow above headings or gradient text.

## Composition

The initial workbench centers a balanced two-line headline and compact upload panel on the black page. The word “soundtrack.” uses Spotify green. The upload panel has a dashed rounded inset containing a green image icon centered in a dark rounded square, the title “It starts with your photo.”, and “Drag an image here, or choose one below.” There is no equalizer illustration or atmospheric hero paragraph. Empty, preparing, and invalid photo states reserve no gallery space. A prepared photo expands the desktop composition into the headline and actions on the left and a square artwork gallery on the right. Brief position transitions move the existing controls into place. On mobile, compact spacing keeps photo actions within the first screen. The artwork gallery follows the controls, with its caption directly below the image. Preparing and ready states keep their status announcements. Drag feedback highlights the upload area while the existing page-wide drop handling remains active. Upload and camera controls share a row. Find my song spans the full control panel width. One concise privacy note sits directly underneath.

The sticky black header uses a text-only Vibe Check wordmark and a named navigation landmark with the Spotify control. Activating the home link resets the current photo and result even on the same route, while modified clicks retain native link behavior. New photo appears when a photo is ready or preparing and runs the same reset. Reset returns keyboard focus to the visible upload button. The header uses a fine divider and wraps its actions on small screens when New photo is present. There are no connection helper lines or filler navigation links.

Spotify feedback appears in a dismissible overlay directly below the header, without moving the workbench or taking focus. One typed notification variant contains either a success or an error message. “Signed in to Spotify” appears only after authentication resolves, including authentication started by individual or batch queueing. Success uses a polite status announcement; errors use an alert. Starting authentication, disconnecting, or resetting clears old feedback. Queueing continues independently of the notification. The footer retains the Spotify Premium and allowlist requirements and the automatic top-match queue behavior.

The artwork gallery shows one unobscured square image at a time, with the title, artist, and confirmed queue status below it. The photo caption contains only “Your moment”. Repeated gallery introductions and instructions are omitted. Images use contain sizing so the whole cover stays visible. A separate horizontal thumbnail row contains the photo followed by confirmed queued songs. Each thumbnail is a native button with a readable title and artist, an accessible name, and a persistent pressed state. Clicking, tapping, Enter, or Space selects the artwork. Hover never changes selection. Missing or failed Spotify artwork falls back to a music icon, verified title, and artist on a neutral dark surface.

The gallery derives from the current recommendation and existing queue states. Finding a song or displaying recommendations adds no album covers. A song appears only after Spotify confirms its queue request. Pending requests, failures without confirmation, and unverified candidates add nothing. Each identity is the request ID plus song ID, deduplicated to one entry per key. Artwork URLs are validated at the Spotify boundary. Comparison selection and disconnecting Spotify leave confirmed entries intact. Local gallery state stores only the selected ID, with the photo as the fallback when that ID is absent. Newly queued songs do not change the current selection. Choosing another photo or resetting removes the gallery immediately, and responses for older requests cannot add covers to the new photo. There is no separate history or persistence. Up to eight songs remain individually selectable through the scrollable thumbnail row on desktop and mobile.

Results use the same dark surfaces. The actual winner and Spotify player lead into a ranked queue selection list. Each candidate has an independent checkbox for queue selection and a title button for comparison. Checkboxes include title and artist in their accessible names. A winner label identifies the server-selected song independently of comparison. Select available excludes unverified, pending, and confirmed songs. Clear removes selection. Add selected shows the count and queues songs sequentially in ranked order. Successful songs leave the selection and display Queued. The first failure stops the batch; failed and remaining songs stay selected for retry. All queue requests are exclusive, including automatic winner queueing and individual adds. Confirmed songs cannot be added again in the current result. Connecting from the header never adds a song; connecting through a queue action resumes only that explicit selection. Changing photo or resetting stops pending batch remainders.

The ranking, mood comparison, and weight explanation form separate sections divided by generous spacing and fine rules. Six parameter cards each show two labeled bars on the same 0–10 scale. White represents the photo, and green represents the song. Both bars retain exact numeric values and shared endpoint labels. Each card states its absolute gap and weighted match points lost. A Photo evidence disclosure preserves the original supporting evidence without crowding the bars. The six normalized weights appear as a simple numeric list with the original reason. Mobile stacks the candidate controls and comparison rows with readable labels.

The raw numeric comparison and scoring formula sit in the lower How we decided disclosure. It opens by default at widths of at least 768 pixels and starts closed on mobile. The main charts remain visible at every width. Scores and ranking always come from the recommendation. Derived match points lost equal 10 × normalized weight × absolute gap. Display rounding never changes ranking. Mood values are Gemma estimates, and similarity percentages do not represent confidence or measured audio.

## Motion and interaction

Artwork selection is persistent and works with pointer, touch, and keyboard. The selected cover has a brief 180-millisecond fade, with its title, artist, status, and pressed thumbnail updated together. The full image remains visible without overlap or perspective transforms. The thumbnail row scrolls horizontally while allowing normal page scrolling. Selecting artwork never changes recommendation or queue state.

Reduced motion removes artwork fading, spatial transitions, and decorative CSS transitions. Borders, color, captions, and pressed states still communicate interaction. The gallery unmounts before revoked photo URLs can linger in an exit animation. Photo preparation, loading, and result transitions use brief fades and optional blur. Chart changes take about 180 milliseconds and can be interrupted by another selection. There are no repeating animations. `MotionConfig` follows the user's reduced-motion preference.

Every control has a visible keyboard focus ring. Selected candidates expose `aria-pressed`. Mood meters retain labels and numeric values. Errors and loading updates keep their live regions. Preserve existing photo, recommendation, cancellation, queueing, and object URL lifecycle behavior.

Confirmed queue entries retain their artwork through comparison changes and Spotify disconnects. Repeat additions are disabled.
